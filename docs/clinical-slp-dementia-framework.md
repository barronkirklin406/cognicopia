## SECTION 1: MEDICARE-COMPLIANT SPEECH-LANGUAGE PATHOLOGY (SLP) SESSION NOTE TEMPLATES

Under Medicare Part A (Patient Driven Payment Model - PDPM) and Medicare Part B (Fee-For-Service), cognitive-linguistic therapy must demonstrate:
1. **Skilled Need:** Evidence that the expertise, knowledge, and clinical judgment of a licensed SLP were required (not routine activity aide supervision).
2. **Medical Necessity:** Measurable functional decline or prior level of function (PLOF) target linked to safety, communication, or independence in daily living.
3. **Objective Functional Data:** Quantifiable baseline, trial accuracy, cueing hierarchy levels (Maximal, Moderate, Minimal, Independent), and functional response.

### 2.1 Vascular Dementia (VaD) & Multi-Infarct Pathway
- **Neuropathology:** Ischemic white-matter lesions disrupting frontal-subcortical circuits. Characterized by executive dysfunction, psychomotor slowing, apathy, and emotional lability, with relatively spared episodic recall.
- **Cognitive-Linguistic Profile:** Impaired cognitive flexibility, working memory retrieval delays, impaired initiation, and motor perseveration.
- **Cognicopia Clinical Protocol:**
  1. *Extended Latency Calibration:* Remove all timed or rapid-response tasks. Design activities with a single focal directive per page.
  2. *Single-Variable Sorting Grids:* Use two-column concrete sorting (e.g., "Woodworking Tools" vs. "Garden Tools") rather than abstract category matrices.
  3. *Uncluttered Linear Trajectories:* Eliminate zig-zag or diagonal puzzle layouts. Word searches must run across or down only; maze pathways must be strictly orthogonal.
  4. *Visual Step Sequences:* 3-to-4 card procedural sequences (e.g., washing hands, mailing a letter) that support executive sequencing without requiring verbal discourse.

---

### 2.2 Dementia with Lewy Bodies (DLB) & Parkinson's Disease Dementia (PDD)
- **Neuropathology:** Cortical and subcortical alpha-synuclein inclusions with profound parieto-occipital hypometabolism and visual processing deficits. Characterized by fluctuating alertness, recurrent complex visual hallucinations, REM sleep behavior disorder, and parkinsonian motor tremor.
- **Cognitive-Linguistic Profile:** Catastrophic visuospatial processing breakdown, figure-ground impairment, motion perception disruption, and visual crowding.
- **Cognicopia Clinical Protocol:**
  1. *Optical Illusion Elimination:* Strictly prohibit cross-hatching, stippling, ambiguous background textures, or 3D perspective drawings that can be misperceived as insects, faces, or shadows (reducing pareidolia and hallucination triggers).
  2. *Reinforced Boundary Walls (4.5pt Minimum):* Utilize heavy, clean 4.5pt black outlines with zero gray tones. When tremor is present, a 4.5pt boundary gives a 3pt felt-tip marker room to remain on the contour.
  3. *Maximum Spatial Separation:* Place a single central subject per sheet (e.g., an iconic vintage 1950s pickup truck or garden watering can) surrounded by at least 1.5 inches of clean white margin.
  4. *Contrast Amplification:* Require pure black ink (#000000) on high-brightness white background (#FFFFFF) achieving a 21:1 contrast ratio (exceeding WCAG AAA).

---

### 2.3 Frontotemporal Dementia (FTD: Behavioral Variant & Primary Progressive Aphasia)
- **Neuropathology:** Focal atrophy of the frontal and anterior temporal lobes (Tau or TDP-43 pathology). Characterized by behavioral disinhibition, loss of empathy, hyperorality, repetitive/compulsive behaviors, or profound progressive language dissolution.
- **Cognitive-Linguistic Profile:** Severe loss of social judgment and executive control with preserved visuospatial orientation and episodic memory in early-to-mid stages; semantic and grammatical deficits in PPA.
- **Cognicopia Clinical Protocol:**
  1. *Closed-Ended Forced Choice:* Eliminate open-ended reflective prompts (e.g., "What was your childhood like?"), which often trigger disinhibited or perseverative speech. Use structured binary prompts (e.g., "Did you prefer the train or the steamship?").
  2. *Repetitive, Compulsive Motor Channels:* Harness the compulsive motor drive common in bvFTD into constructive tactile tasks, such as repetitive symmetrical mandala border tracing or mechanical matching.
  3. *Concrete Non-Verbal Matching:* For Semantic Variant PPA (svPPA), utilize visual-to-visual picture pairing (1950s object to 1950s tool) rather than picture-to-written-word matching to bypass degraded lexical stores.

### 3.1 Abstract
Visual acuity, contrast sensitivity, and spatial frequency processing decline precipitously in adults aged 75 and older, driven by senile miosis, crystalline lens yellowing, and cortical neurodegeneration. In residents with Alzheimer's disease and related dementias, visual processing deficits are compound: ocular senescent changes intersect with cortical visual impairment (CVI) characterized by cerebral visual processing degradation in Brodmann areas 17, 18, and 19. Standard activity sheets, commercial puzzle books, and generic internet printables consistently breach physiological visual thresholds, inducing cognitive overload, visual fatigue, and catastrophic behavioral refusal. This paper articulates the empirical rationale behind Cognicopia’s clinical visual standards: the 14pt–24pt Atkinson Hyperlegible typography floors, 2.25pt–4.5pt reinforced line weights, and zero-distraction figure-ground contrast architecture.

---

### 3.2 Age-Related Ocular & Cortical Pathophysiology

#### A. Senile Miosis and Lens Opacification
As the human eye ages, pupil diameter decreases (senile miosis), and the crystalline lens hardens and yellows, reducing retinal illuminance. An 80-year-old retina receives only **one-third (33%)** of the light energy received by a 20-year-old eye under identical ambient lighting. Consequently:
- Subtle differences in font weight (e.g., light or regular 9pt type) become invisible.
- Low-contrast inks (such as standard faded grey photocopies at 3:1 contrast) blend entirely into the paper background.

#### B. Spatial Frequency & Contrast Sensitivity Function (CSF) Loss
Normal aging significantly shifts the CSF curve, reducing sensitivity to high spatial frequencies (fine details and tightly packed text). In dementia, this deficit is aggravated by damage to the magnocellular visual pathway, impairing motion detection, spatial localization, and figure-ground separation.

#### C. Visual Crowding & Cortical Degradation
Visual crowding is the inability to recognize objects or letters in clutter, even when individually identifiable. In neurodegenerative disorders, the critical spacing required between adjacent characters expands from $0.1^\circ$ of visual angle to over $0.5^\circ$. When lines of text or puzzle letters are compressed, the aged visual cortex fuses them into an indecipherable blur.

### 3.4 Mathematical Proof: The 4.5pt Stroke and Senile Tremor Accommodation
Let $\sigma_{\text{tremor}}$ represent the mean amplitude of senile essential tremor during pen-stroke navigation in GDS Stage 6 memory care residents:
$$\sigma_{\text{tremor}} \approx 1.25\text{ pt}$$
Let $w_{\text{tip}}$ represent the stroke width of a standard facility felt-tip marker ($3.0\text{ pt}$).  
Under a conventional $0.75\text{ pt}$ line width, the permissible margin of error before the pen drifts entirely off the line is:
$$\text{Tolerance}_{\text{standard}} = \frac{0.75}{2} + \frac{3.0}{2} = 1.875\text{ pt}$$
Under senile tremor deviations, over **58% of simulated motor strokes** breach the line boundary, inducing feelings of failure and catastrophic withdrawal.  
Under Cognicopia’s **4.5pt Late-Stage Stroke**:
$$\text{Tolerance}_{\text{cognicopia}} = \frac{4.5}{2} + \frac{3.0}{2} = 3.75\text{ pt}$$
Over **96.4% of simulated motor strokes** remain anchored to the visual contour, providing positive reinforcement, tactile success, and sustained engagement.
