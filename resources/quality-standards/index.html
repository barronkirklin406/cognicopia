Governance & Clinical Pillars

Memory care programming is judged on whether each resident’s activities fit that resident. Cognicopia gives your team three things a surveyor can see on paper: sheets built from the resident’s own life, a dated page for the chart, and print that survives a busy copier room.

Pillar 1 · Clinical

Person-centered, non-pharmacological engagement

  • Built from the resident. Work, hobbies, hometown, the decades they grew up in and the topics to avoid shape every sheet.
  • Matched to the stage. Type is never under 14 pt (early), 18 pt (middle) or 24 pt bold (late); late-stage pages carry one thing to look at, with nothing else on the sheet.
  • Adult dignity. A filter on every page stops quizzing, scoring, baby talk and child-directed words. Subjects are tools, gardens, music, the kitchen and the radio.
  • Evening support. A calm, low-demand sheet is available for the late-afternoon and sundowning hours, with a one-line tip for the person sitting beside the resident.
Pillar 2 · Operational

Copier-ready, zero staff prep

  • Pure black, vector geometry. Lines and type print as 100% black (#000000) on white, with no grey fills to dither or smear across repeated copy generations. Results vary by machine; Tab 3 tests yours.
  • Clipboard and binder safe. Sheets keep a binding gutter on the left (0.75 in on Cognicopia pages; Tab 3 tests that your clipboard clears 0.65 in) and nothing within 0.5 in of any edge.
  • Nothing to prepare. No cutting, folding or assembly. Choose the resident, press print, hand out the sheets.
  • Standard stock. US Letter, single-sided, on the 20 lb bond a facility already stocks.
Pillar 3 · Governance

Independent publisher, local data

  • One accountable publisher. Kirk Barron of Minot, North Dakota reviews the catalog and answers facility questions directly at cognicopia@outlook.com.
  • Nothing leaves the computer. The tools run in the browser. There is no server, account or cloud copy of resident details.
  • Encrypted at rest. Saved details are sealed on the computer (AES-256-GCM) and can be locked with a passphrase. Printed sheets, shared computers and passphrase handling stay under your own privacy program.
  • Procurement-ready. Net 30 invoicing, a vendor information sheet and a W-9 (Tab 4).

Operational durability, in plain terms

  • Pure #000000 vector geometry. Because pages use only black and white, a copier has no tint to screen into dots. Edges stay crisp on the tenth copy of a copy.
  • Heavier lines for unsteady hands. The thinnest line Cognicopia prints is 2.25 pt (early), 3 pt (middle) or 4.5 pt (late). Coloring outlines are 3 pt or heavier.
  • A gutter that clips. The binding edge stays clear so a clipboard or binder ring never covers a word.
  • Built for high-volume copy rooms. Made for the monochrome laser copiers common in care communities (Ricoh, Xerox, Canon, HP and similar). Run the Tab 3 sheet on each machine you use.

    Tab 3 of 4 · Tool B

    Copier Calibration & Margin Test

    One US Letter sheet that shows whether your copier holds the line weights Cognicopia depends on, and whether a clipboard or binder ring clears the gutter. Print it on each machine, then copy the copy once to see what a second generation does.

    • The 0.65 in binding gutter the clipboard test checks
    • Dashed lines: the 0.75 in Cognicopia page gutter and the 0.5 in outer margins

    Run the test

    1. Load plain US Letter. Choose Actual size (100%). Turn off “fit to page”, “shrink oversized pages” and any toner-saver or economy mode.
    2. Scale. Measure the two black rulers: the long one is exactly 9.00 in, the short one 6.00 in. If either is off by more than 1/16 in, the machine is rescaling.
    3. Line weights. Find the finest of the five lines (0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.5, 3.0 pt) that is solid from end to end. Cognicopia’s early-stage floor is 2.25 pt and its middle-stage floor is 3 pt, so 2.5 pt solid gives a little margin and 3.0 pt matches the middle stage. The finer lines show how much more your machine can hold.
    4. Fine pairs. The groups of parallel lines should stay separate. Merged groups mean the machine is spreading toner.
    5. Black and reverse. The black patch should be dense with no streaks, and the white 14 pt words on it should stay readable.
    6. Gutter. Clip the sheet into your clipboard or binder. The shaded 0.65 in strip should hold the clip or rings, with every word still visible.
    7. Second generation. Copy the sheet, then copy that copy. Compare the 2.5 pt line and the fine pairs.

    Marks within about 0.25 in of the paper’s edge may not print on some machines. The test keeps its marks inside 0.5 in for that reason; the punch positions are for reference on screen only.
