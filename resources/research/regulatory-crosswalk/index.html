Regulatory Crosswalk: CMS Requirements for Activities, Dementia Care and Staff Training

The federal rules behind activity programs in nursing homes, and where Cognicopia can help deliver and document them

4 min read7 sourcesReviewed September 30, 2026

Written for: Compliance officers, Administrators and boards, Activity directors

Key takeaways

  • F679 (§483.24(c)): an ongoing program of activities based on each resident’s comprehensive assessment, care plan and preferences.1
  • F744 (§483.40(b)(3)): a resident living with dementia receives the treatment and services needed for their highest practicable physical, mental and psychosocial well-being.2
  • F947 (§483.95(g)): at least 12 hours of in-service training a year for nurse aides, including dementia management and abuse prevention.3
  • Surveyors interpret these rules with CMS’s State Operations Manual, Appendix PP.4
  • Tools can support delivery and documentation. Compliance rests on the facility’s own assessment, care planning, staffing and oversight.

Why a crosswalk1 min

In brief: About half of nursing home residents live with dementia, and several requirements govern what they do each day.

About 46% of nursing home residents live with Alzheimer’s or another dementia, rising to 58% among long-stay residents.5 Their days are shaped by several federal requirements at once: activities, dementia services, care planning, dignity, privacy and staff training. This brief sets them side by side, with where Cognicopia can help.

What the regulations say1 min

In brief: The rule text for activities, dementia services and nurse aide training.

§483.24(c) Activities (F679)

“The facility must provide, based on the comprehensive assessment and care plan and the preferences of each resident, an ongoing program to support residents in their choice of activities, both facility-sponsored group and individual activities and independent activities, designed to meet the interests of and support the physical, mental, and psychosocial well-being of each resident, encouraging both independence and interaction in the community.”1

§483.40(b)(3) Dementia treatment and services (F744)

“A resident who displays or is diagnosed with dementia, receives the appropriate treatment and services to attain or maintain his or her highest practicable physical, mental, and psychosocial well-being.”2

§483.95(g) Nurse aide in-service training (F947)

In-service training must be sufficient to ensure the continuing competence of nurse aides, and no less than 12 hours per year; include dementia management training and resident abuse prevention training; and address areas of weakness found in performance reviews and the facility assessment.3

§483.10(a)(1) Resident rights and dignity (F550)

“A facility must treat each resident with respect and dignity and care for each resident in a manner and in an environment that promotes maintenance or enhancement of his or her quality of life, recognizing each resident’s individuality.”6

What Cognicopia does not do1 min

In brief: It supports the facility’s program; it does not replace assessment, qualified staff or legal advice.

  • It does not replace the comprehensive assessment, the care plan or clinical judgment.
  • It does not qualify anyone as an activity professional (F680).
  • Its Academy certificates are in-service training records, not accredited continuing education, unless a facility enters its own approval statement.
  • It supports a facility’s privacy practices, but it does not by itself make a facility HIPAA compliant.
  • This brief is not legal advice. Check the current regulations and Appendix PP, and your state’s own requirements.

What this means for your community

  • Keep activity assessments, preferences and visual needs current in each resident’s care plan.
  • Plan both group and individual activities, and document participation.
  • Track nurse aide in-service hours through the year, including dementia management and abuse prevention.
  • Read the current Appendix PP guidance for each tag your program touches.

What this evidence does not show

  • Regulations and surveyor guidance change. This brief reflects them as checked on September 30, 2026.
  • State requirements may add to the federal rules.
  • A crosswalk shows where tools can help; it is not a compliance determination.
